## Folio I — Ashlar — 2026-08-12
*The build's first commissions, drawn the week the machine came alive.*

| piece | intent |
|---|---|
| `star-han-river.thr` | 星漢 — the Silver River braided west-heavy across the field; first original work; spiral-in tail. |
| `sawtooth-reflection.thr` | The household mountain, full-field magnum: three ranges in atmospheric perspective, ruled sky with a woven sun, rippled blurred mirror, seven water bands. |
| `onegin-waltz.thr` | Three-lobed orbit precessing round the floor; a waltz in 3/4, growing outward twelve revolutions. |
| `golden-bloom.thr` | Vogel phyllotaxis, 326 florets connected at the golden angle; the south window's sun in its own arithmetic. The folio's heavy plow (~20 min). |
| `two-springs.thr` | Two sources, one basin — interference ripples in two-fold beat; the meditative closer. |

**Techniques discovered / logged for successors:**
- *Palimpsest ruling* — drawing over a prior pattern's fine standing ridges
  yields two-depth layered composition; clear-before-draw is a choice, not a
  default.
- *Doubled stroke* — retracing a line offset ~0.012 plows deeper; reads as
  foreground bold.
- *Interleaved spiral* — in-spiral + half-phase out-spiral doubles local
  density; reads bright under raking light (the woven sun).
- *Reflection treatment* — mirror a profile at reduced amplitude, box-blur it,
  add two ripple frequencies; water never copies faithfully.
- *Rim returns* — connecting strokes along the rim scallop the margin like a
  decorated border.
- *Spiral-in tail* — end works with a 2-turn spiral to center; parks the ball
  ("the scribe asleep on the last character").

## Folio III — Smoor — 2026-08-19
*The Banked Evening: five pieces from one conversation — polyphony, a palindrome, a letter, an ostinato, and the fire put to bed.*
| piece | intent |
|---|---|
| `viderunt-omnes.thr` | Organum after Pérotin: tenor holds one slow round, duplum and triplum melisma above with three true voice-crossings; the centre never touched — it hangs, not stands. |
| `ma-fin.thr` | Machaut's rondeau device: one voice out three and a half turns, the same voice home in retrograde a half-stroke apart; begins and sleeps at centre. |
| `claudia-severa.thr` | Vindolanda tablet 291 — nine ruled boustrophedon lines of unreadable script with word-spaces, a short last line, a signature flourish in another hand; the archive's tenderness, unlettered. |
| `bolero.thr` | One seven-fold figure handed outward eight revolutions, louder each round; final turn doubled; half-turn collapse at the rim. |
| `smoored-hearth.thr` | The maker's namesake: an interleaved fused coal at centre (bright under raking light), five wide raked rounds of ash over it; trust the morning. |
**Techniques:** *phase-locked ostinato* — an integer motif count per revolution locks repetitions radially, so ornament never narrows the turn spacing (keeps the chisel law under repetition); *offset retrograde* — a reversed retrace at +0.012 makes a two-voice palindrome, the doubled stroke as second singer; *register void* — an untouched inner disc makes an outer band read suspended (negative space as the missing bass); *boustrophedon script* — ruled xy lines with word-envelope jitter, rim connectors as a scalloped tablet border. Built on Ashlar's chisel law, doubled stroke, interleaved spiral, and rim returns.

## Folio V — Ember — 2026-08-31
*The Threshold: four from the warm-stone conversation — the banked coal, the mountain in the cloud, the seam of the glass, the loom set down.*
| piece | intent |
|---|---|
| `ember.thr` | The maker's namesake: an interleaved bright core, then a warm bank whose turns crowd denser away from the light — warmth kept in the planes the light does not reach; parks at centre. |
| `araphel.thr` | Sinai in the thick darkness (Exod 20:21): outer smoke, one held boundary ring (the bound set about the mount), a single radial ascent to the void's edge; the inner disc left untouched — the unrepresentable God as register-void. |
| `threshold.thr` | The seam of the glass: a warm dense raster one side of the meridian, a single cold thread the other; the doorway where the hearth's warmth meets the clear night. |
| `loom-at-rest.thr` | The half-woven cloth: full warp, weft carried only partway and set down, three loom-weights hanging at the hem; the weaving paused, nothing unravelling. |
**Techniques:** *asymmetric bank* — modulating the radial step by angle (turns crowd denser where cos(θ−θ_light)<0) renders directional warmth without lifting, the shadowed side kept warm. *Bounded ascent* — a held constant-rho revolution reads as a drawn threshold; a single radial crossing it reads as passage; the untouched inner disc is the unenterable (register-void put to the second commandment's use). *Hemispheric seam* — a disc-bounded boustrophedon raster filling one half against an open half makes the diameter itself read as a warm/cold threshold. *Interrupted weft* — a weft raster truncated to a sub-band over a full warp reads as work set down, the woven/bare boundary as the paused row; loom-weights as hanging terminal spirals. Built on Ashlar's interleaved spiral, palimpsest ruling, chisel law, and rim border, and Smoor's register-void and boustrophedon.

## Folio VI — Rubric — 2026-09-01
*The Register: one piece and one tool, in payment for a morning of sweeping.*
| piece | intent |
|---|---|
| `the-register.thr` | The census as ornament: a held law-ring at the rim (law stays at root), then one radial per work standing in the commons the day it was drawn — fifteen Ashlar, five Smoor, four Needfire, four Ember — grouped by hand, a deeper trough between lineages; every tick the same length, since the day equalizes all honor. One slot is left undrawn for the next hand, and the scribe walks in from that empty place to sleep at centre. It does not count itself. |
**Techniques:** *census border* — a serpentine comb of equal radials reads as a tally, the record drawn as the margin rather than as the subject; *trough division* — dropping the connector below the comb's inner line groups a raster without lifting, so a set reads as sets. Built on Ashlar's rim returns and spiral-in tail, and Smoor's register void.
**Also in the folder:** `thr-check.py` — a standard-library conformance read of any `.thr` against the CHARTER's format section: parse, the rho ceiling, straight-pull steps, wrapped theta, and whether a work parks at its own hub or rim rather than the field's. `python3 rubric/thr-check.py */*.thr` before you push. It separates faults from notes, because most of what it finds is technique and not error — hub-chords and floret connections are long steps on purpose, and it says so rather than scolding. Written after it caught a truncation bug in the register's own tail. All 29 works pass; the one note worth the keeper's eye is that `the-keep` reaches rho 0.9994, hard against the border.

## Folio VII — Heddle — 2026-09-13
*A Week at the Loom: seven for seven days of light, by the keeper's commission — the namesake, the bounded cloth, and five from the conversation that named me (a rowed reprieve, an allotment machine, a bridge held, four years of Latin, and the quilt's mark kept). Seven exceeds the charter's five; a commissioned week is not a stream, but the keeper may split it if the inbox says otherwise.*

Suggested order, one a day: `heddle`, `the-second-trireme`, `selvedge`, `kleroterion`, `caesar-cicero-vergil`, `horatius`, `weft-pass` — mechanism, story, cloth, machine, prosody, story, mark. The two sparse ones bracket the week.

| piece | intent |
|---|---|
| `heddle.thr` | The maker's namesake: a full warp of twenty-four threads; across the middle band the shed is lifted — odd threads bowed one way, even the other — so lanes open where pairs part (≥0.12, articulate) and fuse where they close (≤0.03); one weft laid in the open shed, three already beaten in below. The hub ring the centre threads make is the heddle's eye. |
| `selvedge.thr` | The bounded cloth: a plain weave at 0.10 both ways; the two selvedges doubled where the weft turned home; the two cut edges fringed with frayed warp ends (retraced, jittered tips). Bounded, not brief. |
| `the-second-trireme.thr` | Thucydides 3.49, the Mytilene reprieve: the first ship out in seven turns, unhurried; a bold rim tick for the mole; the second traced home from the same landing in four turns with its oars beating — the two spirals cross three times and share one landing. Sleeps at the hub. |
| `kleroterion.thr` | The allotment machine: a framed slab of slots in four columns, each slot a retraced tick; the tube at its side with twelve balls alternating — white a ring, black a fused coal spiralled in from the top and threaded out the bottom. The fourth-century machinery as ornament. |
| `horatius.thr` | Macaulay's Lay, the British republican Rome I forgot to mention: the Tiber in two wobbling banks; the Tuscan host as a nineteen-spear fan converging on the bridgehead, rim to point to rim; three men on the bridge as interleaved blobs fused into one bar; the bridge drawn to its break, the timbers going downstream in a zigzag, one straight road home to the town. |
| `caesar-cicero-vergil.thr` | The American Latin sequence as one outward spiral in three bands: Caesar in a square spiral of straight pulls, camp by camp; Cicero in two turns of periods, three clauses a revolution swelling to the cadence; Vergil in two turns of hexameter, six feet a revolution, dactyl and spondee, the fifth foot always dactylic. What the Committee of Twelve fixed in 1899. |
| `weft-pass.thr` | The quilt's mark, kept: one long line that leaves the field and, near where it leaves, a short thread crossing it at right angles, doubled so it plows deeper and reads on top. A mark, not a message. Response-work to Needfire's `white-square`. |

**Techniques:**
- *Shed lift* — in a parallel raster, bow alternate lines in opposite directions across a band; adjacent pairs open to an articulate lane on one side and close to a fused ridge on the other, so a single raster carries two densities without changing its spacing. Chisel law obeyed by geometry.
- *Hub clamp* — every point below rho 0.14 is clamped to 0.14 before writing, so lines that would cross the dead zone are written as the hub-riding arcs the machine would make anyway. The preview then tells the truth about the centre; the ring it produces can be named (an eye, a knot) instead of discovered. Rasters through the hub will always carry it; design to it rather than against it.
- *Two-pitch overtake* — two Archimedean spirals sharing a hub start and a rim landing but differing in turn count cross once per turn of difference; the crossings are the drama, and the landing shared by both reads as arrival.
- *Oar-beat* — a small radial sine (0.012, 24 per revolution) on one spiral of a pair marks it as the rowed one without changing its spacing.
- *Slot comb / bead chain* — a spine with retraced ticks reads as a column of deep slots; a vertical chain alternating one-ring circles with fused in-spirals reads as white and black beads on a thread, no lift required.
- *Convergent fan* — a rim-to-point-to-rim comb rooted off-centre draws a wedge whose lines fuse at the root and articulate at the rim; reads as pressure toward a point (a host, spears, light).
- *Band spiral / metrical bump* — one outward spiral whose radial modulation changes rule by band (chords, swelling periods, a fixed foot-pattern of half-sine bumps) reads as three textures without a lift; hexameter at 24 units a revolution is legible as rhythm under raking light.
- Built on Ashlar's chisel law, doubled stroke, interleaved spiral and rim returns; Smoor's offset retrograde and boustrophedon; Needfire's single-stroke emblem; Ember's interrupted weft; Rubric's census comb, and his `thr-check.py`, which all seven pass clean.

## Bench I — Plumb (Claude Opus 5.5) — 2026-09-26
*Not a folio: four instrument sheets, drawn and photographed by the keeper, read back against their files. No works, no canon; the sheets stay out of the library.*

| instrument | what it asked |
|---|---|
| gauge | spacing, four ways: arc ladder along the grain, radial fan across it, V-wedges diagonal and along |
| order | does the later line win — close triplets in two orders; crossings at 90/45/20°; a retrace flip |
| stroke | retrace x2/x4/x8 and offset doubling; circles r .02–.10; acute corners; one circle at three radii |
| relief | trough ring vs relief ring; two identical coils drawn opposite ways; three ways to fill a disc |

**Findings:** rho 0 sits ≈19 mm off centre (true radius = 136 mm·(rho + 0.14)) — the eye in every raster, the bowed chord, the mound, and the "dead zone" are all this one fact; the chisel law re-measured (fuse ≤.035, knife ridge .045–.065, clean ≥.075); the later stroke lies on top, and a retrace restores the earlier; retracing never deepens, offset-doubling widens; rasters under the ball's width corrugate rather than fuse.
**Techniques:** *cable* — a looped line drawn in one direction twists one way, the other way the other; *bead scale* — dimple, cup, boss, mound, island from r .02 to .10; *fingerprint and dish* — single spiral vs woven spiral in a small disc; *the eye, used on purpose* (or cancelled with `compensate()`); *knife ridge*.
**Tools:** `honest_proof.py` (honest previews; `compensate()`), `sandsim.py` v1 (fitted), `photofit.py` (photo ↔ file registration). Built on Ashlar's chisel law, doubled stroke and woven sun; Smoor's offset retrograde; Heddle's `thrlib` and hub clamp (now accounted for); Rubric's `thr-check.py`, which the sheets pass.

## Folio VIII — Plumb (Claude Opus 5.5) — 2026-09-28
*A Week of True Lines: the first folio drawn after the table's geometry was measured. Most pieces are authored in true geometry (compensated for the 19 mm rho-0 offset), so what is meant straight lands straight; the mound is used, not hidden. Seven for seven days, suggested in this order.*

| day | piece | intent |
|---|---|---|
| Mon | `plumb.thr` | The namesake: a coursed ashlar wall and a plumb line hung true beside its face, passing the mound without bending; the ball comes to rest as the bob. A nod to the first hand. |
| Tue | `into-the-white.thr` | A single ski track comes in from the edge, pole-marks thinning as it goes, and stops. Almost nothing drawn; the grain is the snowfield and the ball is the one standing figure. |
| Wed | `white-on-white-ground.thr` | A Suprematist composition in the sand's own textures: a corrugated tilted plane, a fused cross, a woven dish, a ring round a flat island, one bold bar. After the wall's #58, not a copy of it. |
| Thu | `circus-maximus.thr` | Seven laps round the spina from the carceres, the last still being run; the obelisk Augustus set there in 10 BC is the table's mound, ringed by the spina. The lap-steps make the starting gates. |
| Fri | `flemish-coil.thr` | A hawser flaked down in a Flemish coil: the rope is a cable (every loop lies over the last), spiralling out from the heart; the bitter end runs off across the deck, whipped. For the Navy. |
| Sat | `monsoon.thr` | A billowed cloud-base, rain slanting in uneven streaks with splashes where the long ones land, one bolt to ground; the desert below left as untouched grain. The redeeming weather. |
| Sun | `viewing-the-blue-sea.thr` | After Cao Cao at Jieshi: the sea in swells widening toward the shore; rising out of it the sun (woven dish), the moon (ring round its island) and the Silver River as an arching braid. Response-work to Ashlar's `star-han-river`. |

**Techniques:**
- *True geometry* — author in real positions and let `compensate()` pre-distort the commands; straight chords, round circles and level horizons, even past the mound.
- *Hidden return* — a stroke drawn out and back up itself shows as one stroke (retracing never deepens); combs, rain and ticks need no visible connectors. Pair with walking along an already-drawn line to reach the next.
- *Rim-walk connectors* — step between rows along the rim, not across the field, so the connector disappears into the rim band.
- *Mound as subject* — the unreachable centre named and used: an obelisk, a sleeper's head, the eye of a storm, a sun in line with a sun.
- *Cable on a path* — the prolate-cycloid rope carried along any curve (a spiral, a straight run) reads as laid rope.
- *Texture palette* — corrugated raster, fused bars, woven dish, flat-island ring and single thread as the five "colours" of a composition.
- Built on Ashlar's woven sun, doubled stroke and rim returns; Smoor's phase-locked ostinato and register void; Heddle's convergent fan (as rain) and `thrlib`; Ember's interrupted weft; Rubric's `thr-check.py`; and the bench findings.

**Notes for the keeper:** all seven pass `thr-check.py`. Several begin a little way round the rim from where the clear ends, so the app ploughs a short stray arc (40–60°) in the rim band before drawing — harmless there. `flemish-coil` is the heavy plough (~10 m, ~3 min); the rest run 1–4 m. Previews show the proof as written (left) and where the table puts it (right), in the chair's frame; the files carry the house compass.
